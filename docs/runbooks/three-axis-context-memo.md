## Theorem object

The paper's core theorem object is the event package
\[
\mathfrak E = (\mathcal C, \{B_c\}, \sim, \mathcal A),
\]
with admissible global packaging as the central realization problem. The three
axes below are therefore not three coequal theorem objects. They are three ways
of generating or comparing contexts relative to this one theorem object. The
deepest theorem-facing claim remains packaging-level global realization or
failure of realization.

## Mechanism-context

Mechanism-context varies the enable matrix, mechanism family, or control-space
point. It holds fixed, as much as practical, the lens family, packaging policy,
and evaluation regime. This axis supports claims about regime dependence,
package-surface selection, and richer or weaker package structure across
theory-points. It does not by itself support the strongest same-system
contextuality-style claim, because changing the theory-point changes the outer
generative regime rather than only the record algebra or package map on one
fixed support.

This fits the quantum/contextuality paper's language of contexts as strict
extensions and incompatible closures: context change can be real, but the claim
ceiling depends on what is being changed. It also fits the package-change paper's
scope discipline: the strongest theorem claims live on a frozen comparison
domain, not on unconstrained theory-point drift.

Current committed evidence is split. The mechanism campaign summary records a
`design_inadequate` outcome because the bounded family did not meet its full
adequacy floor. At the same time, the committed
`EXP-104 / P6_row_all / n=64 / seed 0` quotient-feasibility witness summary
records an `accepted_proposal_obstruction` on the quotient backend, with
accepted-only infeasibility and natural-pairing feasibility both explicit. So
the honest mechanism-axis statement is: regime dependence is real and one
committed quotient-backed accepted witness exists, but the axis-wide campaign
does not itself close as the strongest same-system obstruction result.

## Lens-context

Lens-context varies the lens or projection family, hence the record algebra used
to generate contexts. It holds fixed the mechanism/configuration, packaging
family as much as possible, and the same support or frozen slice. This axis
supports same-system record-algebra comparison, non-nestedness, strict
extension, and, in the right regime, accepted quotient-backed obstruction.

The quantum/contextuality paper provides the key formal language here:
non-definability on fibers makes context change a strict extension of record
algebra rather than a mere relabeling, and noncommuting closures witness the
structural incompatibility of such changes. The package-change paper adds the
frozen-slice rule: comparison remains legitimate so long as the evaluation
regime is fixed. The Cantor strict-extension paper then explains why changing
the lens can reveal genuinely new packaged strata without changing the
underlying support shell.

Current committed lens evidence has two preserved subregimes. The same-step
bounded campaign under `th4_same_step_negative` remained a negative result with
`candidate_subset_quotient_witness` pressure but no accepted obstruction. The
cross-resolution committed case under `th4_cross_res_all_steps` then produced an
`accepted_proposal_obstruction` with admissible provenance on the same support.
This is why cross-resolution still counts in the accepted lens closure: it is a
strict-extension comparison on a fixed support and fixed evaluation regime, not
a drift to a different ledger.

## Packaging-context

Packaging-context varies packaging operator, packaging family, or selector
branch. It holds fixed mechanism/configuration, fixed primary lens family, and
the same support or frozen slice. This is the strongest axis because it is
closest to incompatible closure or package-map comparison on one fixed theorem
object. In the language of the package-change paper, package change is the
endomap-level locus where distinct package maps can disagree on a fixed domain.

The Cantor strict-extension paper gives the deeper justification: completion
endomaps create packaged-object strata, and genuine extension appears when new
packaged strata become visible that were not definable from the earlier object
map. Packaging-context is therefore the most theorem-facing place to ask whether
one admissible global package exists.

Current committed packaging evidence is the strongest in the repo. The bounded
campaign summary records one `accepted_proposal_obstruction` and two
`candidate_subset_quotient_witness` points, with
`packaging_cross_res_k4_k20` as the accepted flagship. That flagship has
provenance-admissible status, quotient-backed accepted-only infeasibility,
natural-pairing feasibility, and positive `gpd_str`. The caveat is also clear
in the committed row: current packaging variation is still driven by
selector-branch divergence on a fixed operator/family/source surface rather than
a broad multi-family packaging landscape.

## Claim ceiling ladder

Mechanism-context has the weakest claim ceiling. It can support mechanism
dependence and committed witness claims, but not the strongest same-system
obstruction claim from an axis-wide campaign result. Lens-context has a stronger
claim ceiling because it compares record algebras on fixed support; it can
support accepted quotient-backed obstruction when strict extension is still
within one frozen slice regime. Packaging-context has the strongest claim
ceiling because it compares package maps on fixed mechanism, fixed lens, and
fixed support, which is closest to the admissible-global-packaging theorem
object itself.

The committed evidence matches that ladder. Mechanism already has a committed
quotient-backed witness, but not an axis-wide strong campaign result. Lens
closes with an accepted obstruction regime, but only once the strict-extension
cross-resolution regime is admitted explicitly. Packaging currently carries the
strongest accepted axis-wide result, while still preserving the selector-branch
caveat.
